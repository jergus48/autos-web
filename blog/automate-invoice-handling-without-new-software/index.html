How to automate invoice handling without replacing your accounting software

7 November 2026 · 3 min read · invoices, automation, accounting

You do not need to replace your accounting software to stop typing invoices by hand. A small program can sit between your inbox (or scanner) and the system you already use: it reads each invoice, pulls out the details and enters them, and your accountant keeps working in the same tool as before.

What invoice handling looks like today

In many small businesses an invoice arrives by email as a PDF or on paper. Someone opens it, types the supplier, date, number and amounts into the accounting system, checks it against the order, and files the original. It is not difficult, but it is repeated every day and a typing slip can pass unnoticed.

What can be automated

  1. Receiving. Invoices sent to one address are collected automatically, whether they arrive as attachments or as scans.
  2. Reading. The key fields are extracted: supplier, invoice number, date, due date, lines, VAT and total.
  3. Checking. The values are compared with the purchase order or with earlier invoices from the same supplier, and anything odd is flagged: a new bank account, a total that does not add up, a duplicate number.
  4. Entering. The invoice is created in your accounting software through its connection options, ready for approval.
  5. Filing. The original is stored with a clear name, so you can find it in seconds.

A person still approves, at least at first. The program removes the typing and the searching, not the responsibility.

Why you do not have to change your accounting tool

Most accounting systems can receive data from other programs, either directly or through an import file. The automation uses that route. This matters because your accountant, your auditor and your habits stay as they are. The risk of the project stays small, and if you later switch tools, the reading and checking part can be reconnected.

What to check before you start

  • Does your accounting software accept imports or have a connection? Most do. It is worth confirming which one yours offers.
  • How uniform are your invoices? A handful of regular suppliers is easy. Hundreds of different layouts take more care, and clearly written invoices read better than blurry scans.
  • Who approves what? Decide which invoices can go through with one check and which need a second person.
  • Where is the data processed? Invoices contain business and sometimes personal details. Be clear about where they are stored and for how long.

Paper invoices are a separate problem

Paper adds a step: scanning. Once an invoice is scanned, the rest is the same. A further question comes up with an archive of old paper: which of those sheets are you allowed to throw away? We have built a small program linked to a scanner that tells staff which paper invoices can be discarded, which takes a lot of guesswork out of clearing an archive. The retention rules depend on your country and your accountant, so settle those first.

How to measure the result

Count the invoices you handle per month and the minutes each one takes today. After the first month with the automation, count again, and add the corrections your team had to make. If the corrections are rare and the minutes have dropped, you have your answer. If not, the reading step needs tuning before you widen it to more suppliers.

Start with your five biggest suppliers

You rarely need to handle every invoice on day one. Start with the suppliers that send the most, whose invoices look the same each time. They usually make up a large part of the volume, and they are the easiest to read reliably. Add the rest once the first group runs smoothly.
